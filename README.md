# Catspirits · Camera-controlled active games

**A camera-controlled active game: move your body to guide Bit.** Jump, duck and lean sideways through five neon worlds on [catspirits.com](https://catspirits.com). Your browser tracks your movements locally. Camera images stay on your device. English, Hungarian and German are available. Keyboard and touch controls also work with the camera on.

[Motion Arcade – six wand missions](https://catspirits.com/?game=arcade) · [Magyar leírás](README.hu.md) · [Move and play](docs/game.md) · [Neon Blade sword game](docs/sword-tracking.md) · [Neon Pop target game](docs/neon-pop.md) · [Bit Rescue robot game](docs/robot-rescue.md) · [Cloudflare deployment](docs/cloudflare.md)

This repository contains the complete game, its own dependencies, tests and static build. It does not need the original avatar application, an AI account, API keys, a backend, or a local camera helper. The robot, scenery, music and effects are generated by the game.

## Galaxy Tree

Choose **Galaxy Tree** in the game selector to dive through a 3D tree in a sea of stars. Catch sixteen glow caterpillars across eight branches, then find the Starseed on the far side of the trunk. Drag or use the arrows to turn. Click, tap, or use Tab and Enter to catch a visible caterpillar. With camera tracking, pinch over a caterpillar or an arrow. Hold a pinch to turn or spread two pinches to zoom in. AirPods head tracking uses the Tératlasz macOS helper on the local development port 5173 or preview port 4173.

## Run locally

Use Node **24.15.0** (see `.node-version`), then:

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the URL printed by Vite. `/?lang=en` selects English, `/?lang=hu` Hungarian and `/?lang=de` German. The home-screen language buttons remember your choice. Language selection follows the URL, then a saved choice, then the browser's ordered language preferences. The first supported preference is used, with English as the default when none match. Switching language preserves saved progress, scores, colours and sound settings.

## Test and build

```sh
npm run check
npm run preview
```

`check` runs the tests, checks TypeScript and produces **`dist/`**, a self-contained static site. The build uses relative asset paths and works from the domain root or a subdirectory. There are no environment variables to configure.

GitHub Actions runs these checks on pushes to `main` and on pull requests. It does not publish the site.

The existing **catspirits** Worker is connected to this repository and **catspirits.com**. `wrangler.jsonc` deploys `dist` as Workers Static Assets and retains that custom domain. [The deployment guide](docs/cloudflare.md) explains the build settings and local preview.

## Camera and saved data

CatSpirits requests camera access when the page opens and requires it across all games. In Cyber Jumper, choose **Calibrate movement** and stand upright with your shoulders and hips in view before jumping, ducking and leaning to play. The browser processes frames locally using MediaPipe in a worker. Frames stay in memory on your device. The first camera start downloads the pinned MediaPipe runtime and hand and body models from this site. The build checks the original Google models against their SHA-256 hashes.

Camera access requires HTTPS or localhost. The app releases the camera when the page is hidden or closed. If access fails, the camera prompt explains the error and offers a retry.

Progress is stored on the current origin under `catspirits.cyber-jumper.v1`, independently of the original app. Moving from a local preview to catspirits.com starts a separate browser save. There is no account, game analytics, leaderboard or server-side save. Cloudflare provides hosting/security and may process IP addresses and technical request data or set security cookies. The in-game privacy menu explains this distinction.

## Neon Blade — camera sword adventure

[Play Neon Blade](https://catspirits.com/?game=blade) and swing a soft **four-ring pink/green marker wand** through crystals in 60-second rounds. Choose level 1 for free slicing or level 2 for arrows and red spiked hazards. Build combos across three waves, with gold crystals and up to 4× multipliers. Every active crystal responds independently, including multiple hits in one sweep. The mode is linked from Cyber Jumper and has separate local best scores for wand and cursor controls.

The colour tracker runs locally in a worker and reads the shared camera feed during wand play. Native camera pixels and a small scalar WASM kernel accelerate supported devices, with tested JavaScript/canvas fallbacks. [Wand setup and tracking details](docs/sword-tracking.md) · [Puppet2 port notes](docs/puppet2-port.md)

## Neon Pop — hands or wand target practice

[Play Neon Pop](https://catspirits.com/?game=pop): a separate **test game** with 45-second rounds of targets that pop up and expire. Swat them with either hand in camera mode, slice them with the marker wand, or tap with mouse/touch. Both hands and all targets work independently; there is no required cut direction. The countdown ring shows each target's remaining time.

Hand mode tracks visible wrists from the shared body model and needs shoulders, elbows and hands in frame. Choosing the wand starts its colour tracker on the same feed. [Neon Pop setup and controls](docs/neon-pop.md)

## Bit Rescue — pilot a virtual robot arm

[Play Bit Rescue](https://catspirits.com/?game=robot): pilot a 3D robot arm with a camera-tracked shoulder, elbow and hand, mouse, touch or keyboard to deliver three energy cells. By default, close your hand to grab and open it to release. Pinch control is also available. Optional experimental depth control estimates height from calibrated palm size. Hand control to Bit, take over when it gets stuck, and return control without resetting the task. Mission 1 offers clear docking; mission 2 introduces a shield that requires a manual detour. The controller is rule-based, not a trained AI. No demonstrations are recorded, and no physical robot is connected. See [controls, architecture and hardware boundaries](docs/robot-rescue.md).

## Source layout

- `src/core/robot/`, `src/ui/robot/`: Bit Rescue simulation, controller interface, measured elbow retargeting, open/closed-hand or optional pinch control, 3D arm and interface.
- `src/core/pop/`, `src/ui/pop/`: Neon Pop target simulation, hand/wand camera adapter, Canvas arena and interface.
- `src/core/blade/`, `src/ui/blade/`: Neon Blade simulation, wand tracking adapter, Canvas 2D arena and interface.
- `src/core/jumper/`: course simulation, robot rendering, audio and camera adapter.
- `src/core/tracking/`: browser camera worker, body motion input, runner physics and the optional `sword/` extension.
- `src/ui/jumper/`: menus, controls and responsive styles.
- `src/i18n/`: English/Hungarian/German text, language selection and translation tests.
- `public/_headers`, `wrangler.jsonc`: Cloudflare static-site configuration.

Cyber Jumper retains all five worlds, robot colours, jump/duck obstacles, four-section previews, shields, stars, chill mode, reduced motion, music and effects.

Catspirits-owned code is [MIT licensed](LICENSE). Runtime dependencies and the Google pose/hand models retain their own licences; [distributed third-party notices](public/licenses/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt) and full licence texts are included in every build and linked in the game. No third-party character art, music recordings, fonts or AprilTag files are bundled. Source provenance and the limits of the review are documented in [the security review](docs/security-review.md).

[Report security problems privately](SECURITY.md). CI checks dependency vulnerabilities, tests, TypeScript, production build and a Cloudflare deployment dry run. GitHub Actions are pinned to commit hashes; dependency alerts, automatic security fixes and private vulnerability reporting are enabled.

Rendering is limited to at most 60 updates/s in play or camera setup and 30 updates/s in menus. Hidden pages stop rendering, camera and audio. Drawing-buffer resolution is limited to roughly 1080p pixels, including the bloom compositor's buffers. Camera inference still consumes CPU/GPU and battery while active; the game does not promise a device-independent power limit.

### Cyber-electro audio

Cyber Jumper runs on 138 BPM synthwave, Neon Pop on 132 BPM electro-pop, Neon Blade on 148 BPM techno, and Bit Rescue on 128 BPM robotic arpeggios. Each of the six festival missions also has an original backing track. Winning Magnet Meadow triggers a short synth arrangement of the public-domain opening of Beethoven’s Fifth Symphony. The browser generates every note and game effect locally. [Score and arrangement credits](public/licenses/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt)

The **speaker icon** remembers your choice across games. Cyber Jumper also has separate music and effect volume sliders. The shared engine uses one lazy AudioContext, short music lookahead and a 48-voice ceiling. Muting cancels scheduled sounds.

### Hand cursor across the site

The hand cursor starts when you allow the camera. Move an open hand to steer the mirrored cursor. Pinch thumb and index finger to press, release to click, or keep pinching to drag. Hold the cursor at the screen edge for a moment to scroll a menu. A menu pinch goes to the menu while the active game handles its own playfield gestures. In hand cursor control mode, the cursor can also act on the playfield.

The full-screen icon at the top right expands the current play area with its controls and live preview. A pinch fills the browser viewport. A click or tap can use native full screen on supported browsers. Use the icon again or press Escape to return.

Game and language changes within the tab reuse the same video and tracking worker. Returning to the tab resumes the camera. Following a permission denial, use **Restart camera** to try again. Hand loss or a change of control cancels a held press. Open your hand to prepare another pinch.

### Stable pinch clicks and camera landmarks

Pinching anchors the cursor to its last open-hand position, including a short release settling period. Buttons and links remain anchored; canvas/range dragging starts only after a 200 ms hold and a sustained 24 CSS-pixel palm displacement. Tracking loss cancels the press and removes the anchor.

The mirrored live preview stays in the bottom-right corner. It shows tracked body joints and both hands, including finger lines and points. During wand play, it adds the blade and visible rings to the same image. Each set of lines clears when its tracking is lost.

Cyber Jumper and Bit Rescue use original voxel-style geometry: blocky characters and robot links, stepped scenery and a tiled robot workspace. Jumper scenery keeps its existing instanced batches; the 100 robot floor tiles share one instanced draw. The visual changes do not alter collision rules, arm retargeting or camera inference.
